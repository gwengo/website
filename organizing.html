community organizing · NYU ITP / IMA

Black Interactive

A student-run community making space for Black students in creative technology.

Black Interactive celebrates Black identity, amplifies members’ stories, honors Black contributions to art and technology, and showcases contemporary artists through installations, events, workshops, and celebrations.

I lead the group. My work includes:

  • Programming: exhibitions, public programs, and gatherings, including Future Legends and Black Ecologies
  • Fundraising: lead writer for the group’s grants
  • Communications: writing for the community and its partners
  • Network building: alumni mentorship and outreach to other universities, including Harvard GSD’s design conference

exhibition & public program · 2026

Black Ecologies: Land, Memory, and the More-than-Human

A process-based exhibition at the LMCC Arts Center on Governors Island, part of NYU Climate Week.

I proposed the project and co-organized it with members of Black Interactive. The exhibition featured work by Ryan Webber, STAR / Irawo Ajasin, Anatola, and me.

My work covered artist coordination, the curatorial statement, wall text, Open Studios programming, and a two-part zine that served as artist book and visitor guide.

[image: the room, a workshop, or the panel]

public program · June 2026

BYOD: Bring Your Own Data

A one-day public event treating personal data as creative material.

I founded and led production of a full day at 370 Jay Street, Brooklyn: a panel of five speakers, four parallel workshops, and a closing talk, with 103 attendees. Sofia Garcia co-organized.

I wrote the winning $5,000 NYTechWeek grant proposal (a16z). I built the website, information architecture, visual identity, and participant materials, and managed speaker outreach, venue logistics, and sponsor communications. Attendees gave it a 9/10 recommendation rating.

Looking back: A sponsorship conflict surfaced 48 hours before the event and forced a late change to branding and framing. The program stayed intact. Next time I would say sooner that the room matters more to me than the logo.

[image: the Clive Davis Gallery exhibition]

exhibition & public program · Nov 2025 to present

Future Legends

A student exhibition built on a curatorial argument that moves from historical recovery to speculative futures.

A month-long public exhibition at NYU’s Clive Davis Gallery in four themes: Excavate, Dream, Remix, Actualize. Curated with Ryan Webber.

I wrote the 12-page proposal that started the exhibition, secured $14,000+ across NYU department funds, SGA, and CMEP, co-developed the curatorial framework, and supported five events reaching 547 ticketed attendees and 438 subscribers.

Looking back: Actualize was the hardest theme to program. It is easier to ask people to excavate grief than to imagine what comes after it. I would give that theme more room and more of my attention.
